MULTI-AGENT PAPER-SUPPLY ORDER SYSTEM
Design and evaluation notes

Framework and execution model
--------------------------------
The implementation uses smolagents 1.26.0 agent and delegation constructs. It
stays within the five-agent limit: a ProjectOrchestrator ToolCallingAgent with
four managed ToolCallingAgent specialists for inventory, quoting, fulfillment,
and internal business advice. The Customer Advocate role is integrated into
the orchestrator. Each specialist registers a business-action tool that calls
the existing deterministic domain method; pricing, feasibility, reservations,
and transaction validation remain in those methods.

ProjectOrchestrator.handle retains the deterministic business policy and asks
its smolagents manager agent to delegate each specialist action. The manager
delegates through smolagents' managed_agents mechanism; each specialist then
executes its registered tool through the ToolCallingAgent run loop. A local
DeterministicWorkflowModel implements the smolagents Model interface and maps
structured workflow envelopes to framework tool calls. It does not provide
free-form language reasoning or choose business policy. This keeps the full
framework execution path testable without sending customer sample text or
credentials to an external model service. The system makes no LLM or external
supplier-service calls.

The editable workflow diagram is Agent_workflow.mmd; Agent_workflow.png is its
rendered submission image. The internal Business Advisor remains a distinct
managed specialist and is called only after the sample evaluation.

Agent responsibilities and data flow
-------------------------------------
1. ProjectOrchestrator parses a customer's text into item quantities, rejects
   unsupported catalog variants, identifies a stated deadline, and sequences
   the specialists. It returns the quote, pending-approval result, fulfillment
   confirmation, or a specific rejection reason. Its integrated Customer
   Advocate role builds a brief from the supplied job, event, requested order
   size, explicit deadline, and any stated price sensitivity. It shares those
   terms with InventoryAgent and QuoteAgent instead of inventing a budget or
   customer preference.

2. InventoryAgent owns stock and replenishment feasibility. It checks
   inventory, accounts for its in-memory future reservations and reorder
   commitments, estimates local replenishment dates, and ensures the order's
   replenishment cost fits the available cash. It reserves inventory only for
   accepted future-dated orders.

3. QuoteAgent owns catalog-price calculation, historical quote lookup, and
   volume discounts. The current discount schedule is 5% for 500–1,499 total
   units, 8% for 1,500–4,999, and 10% for 5,000 or more; smaller orders receive
   no discount. Historical quotes are context only; the price calculation
   remains based on catalog prices.

4. OrderFulfillmentAgent handles accepted orders. It rechecks stock before
   writing transactions, asks InventoryAgent to record planned replenishment,
   allocates the quote discount over the lines, and records inventory sales
   for the planned fulfillment date while recording customer cash at acceptance.
   Planned restock inventory and its cash cost are dated at estimated arrival.

5. BusinessAdvisorAgent runs after the sample evaluation, reads aggregate
   transaction and inventory signals, and writes internal operational
   recommendations to business_advisor_report.txt. This report is not included
   in customer-facing responses.

Flow: customer request -> ProjectOrchestrator ToolCallingAgent and integrated
Customer Advocate -> smolagents managed delegation to the Inventory
specialist -> managed delegation to the Quote specialist -> (if purchase
intent is explicit) managed delegation to the Fulfillment specialist ->
orchestrator response to the customer. When the requested deadline is
infeasible but a later estimated date is feasible, the orchestrator delegates
the revised feasibility and price check and offers the later date. The
customer must explicitly approve that alternative; the system does not
record an order for it. A request without purchase intent gets a quote marked
quoted_pending_approval. The sample runner is single-turn and does not persist
a quote for acceptance in a later run. After evaluation, the manager delegates
aggregate results to the internal-only Business Advisor; advisor output is
written to business_advisor_report.txt and is not exposed in customer replies.

In an interactive terminal, TerminalProgressDisplay builds a staged progress
bar from completed customer-context, inventory, quote, and fulfillment/offer
outputs. It is silent in non-interactive runs and adds no progress text to the
customer response or CSV evaluation.

The supplier helper only estimates a date from a local quantity-to-lead-time
rule. It is not a live supplier connection and does not transmit a purchase
order. A stock_orders transaction models planned replenishment in the local
ledger; it is not confirmation from a supplier.

Starter functions and helper usage
----------------------------------
generate_sample_inventory(paper_supplies, coverage, seed)
  Generates reproducible inventory rows from the catalog. init_database calls
  it to seed the project's local database.

init_database(db_engine, seed)
  Creates/resets the local SQLite tables and loads generated inventory and
  supplied quote history. The evaluation runner calls it at the beginning.

create_transaction(item_name, transaction_type, quantity, price, date, cash_date)
  Inserts a validated stock_orders or sales row and returns its ID. Inventory
  movement is recorded at date; cash effects are recorded at cash_date (which
  defaults to date). transaction_tool wraps it for the agents; fulfillment
  records the accepted sale on the planned delivery date and its cash receipt
  on the acceptance date.

get_all_inventory(as_of_date)
  Returns positive net stock by item as of a date. inventory_snapshot_tool
  exposes it to InventoryAgent.

get_stock_level(item_name, as_of_date)
  Returns net stock for one item. item_stock_tool exposes it to InventoryAgent
  for planning and to OrderFulfillmentAgent for pre-write validation.

get_supplier_delivery_date(input_date_str, quantity)
  Estimates a delivery date using the starter's quantity-based lead time.
  supplier_eta_tool exposes the estimate to InventoryAgent.

get_cash_balance(as_of_date)
  Calculates customer receipts less stock-purchase payments through a date,
  using each transaction's cash_date.
  cash_balance_tool exposes it to InventoryAgent's affordability check.

generate_financial_report(as_of_date)
  Calculates cash, inventory value, total assets, itemized inventory, and top
  sellers. financial_report_tool wraps this helper as a smolagents tool. The
  evaluation runner uses the tool before and after requests; the InventoryAgent
  also requests a report for large orders (1,000+ units).

search_quote_history(search_terms, limit)
  Finds similar historical quote rows. quote_history_tool exposes it to
  QuoteAgent; results are summarized in the customer-facing quote.

Project-specific tools and decision helpers
--------------------------------------------
inventory_snapshot_tool -> get_all_inventory: date-specific stock snapshot.
item_stock_tool -> get_stock_level: per-item planning and validation check.
cash_balance_tool -> get_cash_balance: affordability input.
financial_report_tool -> generate_financial_report: internal financial and
  inventory health report.
supplier_eta_tool -> get_supplier_delivery_date: local ETA estimate only.
quote_history_tool -> search_quote_history: relevant quote-history context.
transaction_tool -> create_transaction: local stock_orders and sales ledger.
transaction_analysis_tool -> SQLite transaction aggregates plus get_stock_level:
  internal sales, replenishment, top-item, and low-stock analysis for the
  Business Advisor specialist.

BusinessActionTool registers each specialist's existing business operation
as a smolagents tool. DeterministicWorkflowModel produces native smolagents
tool calls from the structured task supplied by ProjectOrchestrator; the
manager agent invokes each ToolCallingAgent specialist through managed_agents.
The custom model is a local workflow adapter, not a mocked agent call: both
manager and worker ToolCallingAgent.run loops execute for every delegated
action in the full sample evaluation.

parse_requested_items extracts quantities and descriptions from the sample
request wording. resolve_catalog_item maps descriptions conservatively to
known catalog names and rejects unsupported sizes/specifications rather than
inventing variants. requested_deadline parses an explicit month/day/year
deadline, otherwise using the request date.

The Customer Advocate role in ProjectOrchestrator._prepare_customer_brief
passes supplied job/event/order-size context, explicit deadlines, and explicit
price-sensitive language to the worker agents. If InventoryAgent reports a
deadline conflict, the orchestrator retries the plan at the latest local ETA
and presents a priced later-date offer when feasible. It never treats that
counter-offer as accepted.

InventoryAgent.assess builds a feasible stock/reorder plan and reports
shortages, timing, and cash constraints; the Inventory specialist exposes it
through its framework action tool. commit_reorders writes planned local
stock_orders transactions after acceptance. reserve_order tracks future
reservations within the current evaluation run.

QuoteAgent.create_quote looks up history, totals catalog prices, and applies
the stated unit-volume discount schedule.

OrderFulfillmentAgent.complete rechecks stock, commits planned restocks,
records sales with the discount allocated across lines, and reserves future
inventory.

ProjectOrchestrator.handle coordinates parsing, catalog checks, planning,
quoting, purchase-intent handling, fulfillment, and the response. The
run_test_scenarios function initializes the database, executes the supplied
requests in date order, records financial snapshots and outcomes, prints the
final report, and writes test_results.csv. It delegates the aggregate analysis
to the Business Advisor specialist and writes the separate internal
business_advisor_report.txt.

Evaluation
----------
Dataset: quote_requests_sample.csv (20 requests).
Result: 4 fulfilled, 12 rejected, 1 quoted_pending_approval, and 3
quoted_alternative_pending_approval offers.
The minimum acceptance criteria are met: at least three fulfilled orders and
at least one clearly explained unfulfilled request. Four accepted sample
orders each changed the cash balance by the quoted total on the request date.

Fulfilled request IDs: 5, 6, 11, and 12. Their quoted totals were $128.25,
$73.15, $49.40, and $137.75. Request 1 received a $65.00 quote and remained
pending approval because it did not express purchase intent. Requests 10, 16,
and 18 received priced offers for later delivery dates (April 12, April 17,
and April 18 respectively); these remain unaccepted offers and do not record
sales or restock transactions.

The remaining rejections explain that requested sizes/specifications are not
in the available catalog. In particular, the system rejects A3/A5 variants
rather than silently substituting another size. The later-date counter-offers
show how the integrated Customer Advocate can negotiate around a missed
deadline without pretending the original schedule is feasible. The supplied
data exercises catalog validation, mixed stock/reorder planning, deadline
negotiation, discounts, quote history, pending approval, and transaction
recording.

The final date-based financial report for 2025-04-17 reports cash of
$45,278.50 and inventory value of $4,701.05. Customer payment is booked when
an order is accepted, while inventory sales and planned replenishment are
dated at fulfillment/estimated arrival. This keeps the available-stock
timeline separate from the cash timeline. All four fulfilled requests (5, 6,
11, and 12) increase cash on their request dates by their quoted totals.
Rows 14 and 15 show cash decreases of $42.00 and $127.75 because restock costs
from earlier accepted requests reach their estimated dates then; those rejected
requests themselves record no transactions. The CSV documents both the cash
timing and these delayed costs.

The internal Business Advisor reported 11 sales transactions totaling
$388.55, identified Cardstock as the leading recorded seller, and flagged
Glossy paper and Cardstock below their configured minimum levels. It also
recommended reviewing catalog demand after 12 unsupported-specification
rejections and validating supplier lead times after three deadline conflicts.
These are internal findings, not customer-facing information.

Strengths and limitations
-------------------------
Strengths: all five runtime agents are smolagents ToolCallingAgent instances,
and each manager-to-specialist action in the full sample run uses framework
managed-agent delegation and a specialist-registered tool. Deterministic
business rules remain behind those tools. Rejections include specific
reasons; unsupported products are not silently substituted; and future
stock/cash commitments are considered during planning. Requests 10, 16, and
18 receive an explicit later-date price offer instead of silently ignoring
the customer's deadline, and the customer has to approve the changed date
before fulfillment.
The interactive terminal progress display reveals completed agent stages;
the Business Advisor's aggregate findings remain separate from customer
responses. The local workflow model keeps the framework execution reproducible
without external requests, but intentionally does not use an LLM for free-form
planning or natural-language routing.

Limitations: item parsing is rule-based and tailored to the supplied request
patterns; the catalog has no A3/A5 or several requested specialty variants;
the local ETA is not supplier confirmation; future reservations exist only in
memory and do not coordinate multiple application processes; and a pending
quote or later-date counter-offer cannot be accepted in a later turn by the
current runner. DeterministicWorkflowModel accepts structured requests from
the business orchestrator rather than deciding which specialist is needed
from free-form text. The sample set still has 12 rejections, primarily because
the requests ask for product sizes/specifications not represented in the
catalog.

Suggested improvements
----------------------
1. Add a persisted quote/order state machine so customers can accept or revise
   a quote in a later turn, with idempotency keys and transaction-safe stock
   reservation.
2. Integrate an authenticated supplier API and purchase-order workflow; replace
   the local lead-time estimate with supplier availability, confirmed dates,
   and explicit failure handling.
3. Expand catalog matching with structured product attributes and targeted
   clarification questions, then add catalog entries only when product
   specifications and pricing are approved.
4. Add focused automated tests for parsing variations, boundary discount
   tiers, cash exhaustion, deadline edges, duplicate requests, and rollback
   behavior after a transaction failure.
5. Validate Business Advisor recommendations against a longer operating
   history and track whether inventory or catalog changes actually improve
   fulfillment rate, lead times, and revenue.
